Position Sizing: The Habit That Keeps You in the Game

Risk a fixed percent per trade and survive the losing streaks.

Most traders focus on picking winners and ignore size. Position sizing decides how much a loss hurts. The rule is simple: choose the percent of your account you accept losing (often 0.5–2%), then divide the dollar amount by the distance between entry and stop.

Example: a $25,000 account risking 1% has $250 at risk. With a $3 stop distance you buy 83 shares.

A 50% loss needs a 100% gain to recover, which is why capping each loss matters more than any single idea.

Stops can gap past your price, so treat the calculation as a ceiling on planned risk, not a guarantee.
