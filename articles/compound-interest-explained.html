How Compound Interest Really Works

Why time in the market matters more than the size of your first deposit.

Compound interest means you earn returns on your earlier returns. Investing $300 a month at 7% for 20 years puts in $72,000 and grows to roughly $156,000, because the later years compound a much larger base.

The two levers you control are how early you start and how steadily you contribute. Waiting ten years often costs more than doubling the monthly amount.

Fees work the same way in reverse. A 1% annual fee quietly removes a large slice of the final balance over decades.

Returns are never guaranteed: use conservative assumptions and test several rates.
